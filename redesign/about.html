About

A WordPress story, in four chapters.

Marine, developer, Southerner, husband. Founder of All-in-One SEO Pack, CEO of Semper Fi Web Design, co-author of WordPress All-in-One for Dummies — and someone who still believes in a small, personal web.

I grew up in the South, studied at Virginia Tech, and signed with the United States Marine Corps — not because any of it was a plan, but because each one felt like the right next door. That's more or less how the rest of this has gone, too.

Virginia Tech & the Marines

College landed me in Blacksburg, Virginia, where I spent a few formative years before leaving school to enlist in the Marine Corps. "College dropout" and "Marine" are both true; the second one mattered more. The Corps gave me a set of habits — show up, keep your word, take the hill — that everything since has rested on.

I still have friends from both. I still go back to both.

Semper Fidelis isn't a slogan. It's a way of making things — software included — that you'd still be proud of ten years from now. — On why the agency is named what it is

All-in-One SEO Pack

In 2007 I released a small WordPress plugin called All-in-One SEO Pack. It was the first SEO plugin for WordPress. I did not expect it to become what it became. Over the next decade-plus it was installed on millions of sites, translated into dozens of languages, and sustained by a community of contributors I'm still grateful for.

In time I handed the plugin to a new team at AIOSEO who could take it further than I could alone. Letting go of something you built is its own kind of work. I recommend it.

Semper Fi Web Design

Alongside the plugin, I ran a WordPress studio — Semper Fi Web Design — building custom sites for small businesses, authors, and non-profits. Veteran-founded, small-team, no-drama. We're still at it.

The book, the talks, the WordCamps

In 2011 I co-authored WordPress All-in-One for Dummies with Lisa Sabin-Wilson — a thick, honest reference for people running real sites. I've since spoken at WordCamps, organized a few, contributed translations, and done the quiet maintenance work that keeps an open community alive.

The open web is worth defending. Write on your own site. Own your archives. Syndicate elsewhere if you want to — but come home.

Photography, travel, family

Outside of software, I travel when I can and photograph more than I publish — mountains, streets, the occasional yak. My wife Kim runs Yak My Body, a social enterprise that supports marginalized Tibetan women through soap-making. We spend time in Asia most years because of her work, and because it's worth it.

Now

These days I write, build, consult selectively, and keep a small personal corner of the internet — this one. If you'd like to be in touch, the contact page is probably the right door.
